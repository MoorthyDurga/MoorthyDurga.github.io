Summer Research at the Vanderbilt Institute for Surgery and Engineering (VISE)


Over the summer, I worked on an eye-tracking study analyzing expert and novice visual behavior during augmented reality–guided kidney procedures. My work focused on processing and classifying gaze data to understand attention patterns, task performance, and the effect of AR guidance. I presented this research at multiple poster sessions including the VINSE (Vanderbilt Institute of Nanoscale Science and Engineering) poster session highlighting my preliminary findings on fixation clustering, guidance effects, and expertise differentiation.

Biomedical Design Lab

As part of a student-run-design laboratory using iXTA EMG equipment, my team of 4 designed and conducted an independent experiment to analyze calf muscle activity during flexion. I performed data collection, signal processing, and statistical analysis to study muscle activation patterns and fatigue behavior. I also completed literature reviews, contributed to our research presentation writing, and presented our findings to peers, connecting the results to broader applications of EMG in prosthetics, rehabilitation, and human performance analysis.

Rapid Prototyping
3D Printed Syringe Pump

Digital Fabrication

In this project, my team of three designed and fabricated a cost-efficient syringe pump within a 4-week timeline as an affordable alternative to commercial models that typically cost several hundred dollars. We developed custom CAD models in Fusion 360 optimized for lightweight PLA construction and cost-effective ABS components where heat resistance was required. Using an Arduino, we programmed control of a NEMA 17 stepper motor via an A4988 (microstepping) driver using the AccelStepper library, converting user-specified flow rates into precise stepper commands while managing timing constraints to maintain accuracy. The electrical system included custom wire harnesses with proper terminations connecting a latching push button for operation control, a limit switch for end-of-stroke detection, and an RGB LED for status indication, all powered by a Mean Well quad-output power supply and protected within a custom enclosure. The design achieved compatibility with multiple syringe sizes through parametric calculations that accounted for syringe diameter and lead screw pitch, ultimately delivering accurate volumetric flow rates during testing.

FRC Robotics


Compliant Wheel Indexing

FRC Rapid React

Created a vectored, deployable intake and indexing system with limited space and multi-part stowing capabilities. Experimented with various materials such as 2-in compliant wheels (green wheels), mecanum vectored intake wheels, and gas shocks. Throughout the prototyping process, I worked around various design constraints, such as the intake being unable to handle head-on impact at high speeds, and the stowing system being vulnerable to side impact when deployed.

The intake was required to manipulate game pieces in a non-destructive way, but it also needed to lift pieces over the exterior bumper to transfer into the indexing system.

Replicated a vendor's telescoping linear actuating system to fabricate in house at a fraction of the cost (pictured left). Integrated custom hooks for game application and cut >10 lbs of weight from original pneumatic design.


When creating the intake pictured below, I experimented with game piece compression using different shaped compliant wheels (larger red 4 inch, cut edges) for better contant with square game pieces. I also tested numerous materials to find an acceptable trade off between flexibility, grip, and durability.

FRC Charged Up

Throughout the cube and cone intake design process, I designed rough prototypes using existing lab materials quickly and cheaply. Pictured above is the initial 3D printed cone intake. Based on testing observations, the components were modified and re-tested. For example, the cone intake grabbers were too grippy, making the slide-on intake difficult. To alter the 3D printed parts, I used acetone to smooth down the exterior of the rounded ball end, which significantly decreased friction between the intake and the rubberized material of the cones, improving its functionality.

Personal Projects

Rotary Joint


As a summer project I designed a 3D-printable rotary joint with a 30:1 gear ratio. I utilized heat set inserts for easy mounting, and a belt and pulley system to allow the center of the joint to be hollow and enable dual ended attatchment for robotic arm applications. I tested the strength of the PLA body (polylactic acid, a standard thermoplastic material used in 3D printing) by conducting stress analysis and experimenting with boning along the underside of the body in weaker areas. I ended with a light, cheap and easily customizable design for a robotic rotary joint.

Biomedical

Worked with a team of biomedical engineering peers to address the problem of college students getting roofied (being unknowingly drugged via drink tampering). We created a wearable device using an Arduino compatible TDS (total desolved soilds sensor to create a ring that lit up when a roofie was detected in any observed drink. We used the SparkFun RedBoard and Arduino to test our ring prototype in a 3-week design module, and ended with a functional ring that accurately detected a change in TDS by lighting up an attached LED on the ring to alert the user of a dissolved substance.
